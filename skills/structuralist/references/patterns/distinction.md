# Pattern: Distinction

## Lens

A natural-language system becomes intelligible through distinctions: what is
treated as the same, what is treated as different, and why that difference
matters now. A distinction may appear within a paragraph, between sections,
between documents, or only in how readers interpret them.

## Tension

Differentiation can clarify meaning, responsibility, audience, or change. It
can also create artificial boundaries, duplicated explanation, and maintenance
cost. Composition can restore coherence, but may collapse differences the
project still needs.

## Questions

- Which meanings are being confused, duplicated, or forced together?
- Are elements being grouped because they share a medium even though they play
  different roles in the project?
- Which apparent differences no longer affect understanding or change?
- What force makes a distinction useful: semantics, responsibility, lifetime,
  cadence, audience, risk, or something project-specific?
- Do the meanings need independent authority, review, disclosure, or
  realization, or would clearer internal structure preserve one coherent
  owner?
- Is open interpretation being confused with settled commitment, or is an exact
  realization being treated as if it owns the meaning that produced it?
- Would the distinction remain useful if the current filenames and vocabulary
  disappeared?

## Possible movements

Differentiate, combine, clarify, rename, narrow, broaden, or deliberately leave
an ambiguity visible. A movement may change prose without changing files, or
change file boundaries without changing the underlying conceptual structure.

Distinguish a project's natural-language system from its artifacts by what
they do in that project, not by whether both happen to use natural language.
Preserve overlap when one carrier genuinely serves both functions.

Distinguish degrees of settlement and realization only where they change how
meaning should be interpreted or edited. “Interpretive” and “formalized” are
not file types, quality ranks, or a binary maturity ladder.

Treat structural overload as a distinction pressure only when meanings forced
together must be interpreted or changed independently. Clarify or recombine a
false distinction; separate a real one at the smallest boundary that preserves
its authority and consequences. Length and density establish neither case.

Do not equate more distinctions with maturity or fewer distinctions with
simplicity. Prefer the arrangement that makes the relevant difference legible
without pretending that every meaningful difference needs its own artifact.
