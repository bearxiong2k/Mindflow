# Pattern: Transformation

## Lens

Every intervention changes not only prose but the system of distinctions,
positions, relations, perspectives, and temporal commitments around it.
Diagnosis observes pressures on that system; construction, migration,
refactoring, and trimming are possible transformations of the same kind.

Healthy evolution often combines two movements. Meaning first expands where
its uncertainty can be held; as it stabilizes, its consequences are absorbed
into progressively more exact owners and formal realizations. Trimming then
releases provisional residue after semantic conservation rather than competing
with expansion from the beginning.

## Tension

Preservation protects continuity but can preserve drift. Transformation can
restore fitness but may change meaning, authority, or project direction. A
small edit can have broad semantic effects, while a large textual diff can be
structurally neutral.

Expansion without absorption populates interpretive owners with settled detail,
duplicate commitments, and expired coordination. Absorption without expansion
prematurely closes interpretation. Trimming without conservation deletes
meaning; conservation without trimming leaves the movement incomplete.

Overload repair can fail in both directions. Refusing a needed separation
keeps independent meanings coupled; splitting from size or aesthetic pressure
fragments one coherent owner. The transformation follows the semantic force,
not a preferred operation.

## Questions

- What is changing in the project, and what change in the natural-language
  system—if any—would remain coherent with it?
- Which meaning should survive, change, appear, or disappear?
- Is this pressure new enough to require expansion, settled enough to require
  absorption, or already absorbed enough to release residue?
- Does this owner contain meanings that must change independently, and would
  clarification, recombination, separation, or repositioning best preserve
  their consequences?
- What exact downstream consequence must now be carried, and which durable
  upstream insight must remain?
- Does lower-level evidence require an explicit upstream reform, or only a
  correction within the existing authority?
- What other elements would this movement reposition or reinterpret?
- Is the proposed intervention reversible, and does reversibility matter here?
- Which consequences follow from evidence, and which require an authority
  choice the task has not supplied?

## Possible movements

Expand, differentiate, stabilize, create, reframe, formalize, migrate, split,
merge, absorb, reconcile, reopen, trim, regenerate, or retire. Choose movements
in relation to the observed forces rather than a preferred workflow or target
shape. They are composable rather than mandatory phases. Conserve meaning that
remains needed; deliberately change meaning when the authorized task requires
it.

After absorption, trim duplicate obligation, expired coordination, and
misplaced realization detail while retaining governing purpose, rationale,
evidence, and unresolved choices in their proper owners. Do not optimize for a
word count or force meaning downward after additional exactness stops helping.

For overload repair, first conserve each meaning and its authority; then
clarify or recombine false distinctions, or split and reposition real ones;
reconnect governing, peer, and realization relations; finally remove duplicated
obligation created or exposed by the movement. A new file is only one possible
carrier for that result.

Analysis may end with no structural change. When mutation is authorized, keep
the change reviewable, preserve unrelated work, and surface consequential
choices that cannot be resolved from the project's available authority.
