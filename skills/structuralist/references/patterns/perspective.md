# Pattern: Perspective

## Lens

The same project subject may be interpreted through different questions,
audiences, levels of precision, or practical concerns. These perspectives can
overlap without being duplicates when each makes a distinct way of seeing
explicit.

## Tension

Flattening perspectives can hide meaningful disagreement or force unlike
readers through one representation. Separating them can produce competing
accounts, artificial territories, or translation cost.

## Questions

- What question or reader treatment gives this perspective its identity?
- Are two accounts genuinely different views or merely repeated conclusions?
- Where must perspectives agree, and where may they remain plural?
- Does one perspective refine, explain, challenge, or realize another?
- Is a translation boundary needed, or would shared prose remain clearer?

## Possible movements

Layer, translate, reconcile, juxtapose, specialize, synthesize, or preserve
plurality. Profiles, readable semantic accounts, exact specifications, and
explanatory views are possible realizations, not prescribed role families.

When one perspective is authoritative over another, make that relation clear.
When none is authoritative, do not create a hierarchy merely to simplify the
diagram.
